Privacy policy

Jarvis has one user: Adam Weir, who built it and runs it on his own computer.

Google data Jarvis can reach

  • Google Calendar: it reads events on the calendars Adam can see. It adds, moves, or deletes an event only on his main calendar, and only after he approves that change.
  • Gmail: it searches and reads Adam's email when he asks about it. It saves a draft, sends, archives, labels, marks read or unread, or moves email to the trash only after he approves each one. It never permanently deletes email.
  • Keeping the inbox tidy: to suggest rules (for example, "archive a store's emails he never opens"), it looks at senders, Gmail's category, and whether he opened their mail; it never reads message text for this. A rule only acts after Adam turns it on, and each day's tidying waits for his approval.
  • Gmail filters: a rule Adam has approved many times in a row can become a Gmail filter, only after he approves that filter. Its filters only archive or label a sender's mail; they never delete, forward, or change other settings. He can remove any filter in Gmail or in Jarvis.

How it's used

Only to answer Adam's own requests and to carry out the actions he approves. Google data is never sold, never used for advertising, and never used to train an AI model.

Where it goes

  • It isn't stored. Jarvis looks things up live when Adam asks and doesn't keep copies of his email or calendar. In any answer that read email or calendar text, Jarvis refuses to save anything to its memory. Its approval log records only the kind of action and its outcome (for example, "calendar create, approved"), never titles, addresses, or message text. The one exception is its list of inbox rules: the names and addresses of mailing-list senders and what to do with their mail, kept on Adam's computer. No personal contact is ever on it.
  • To write an answer, the relevant text is sent over an encrypted connection to Anthropic's Claude API, the AI model that writes Jarvis's replies. To suggest labels, Jarvis sends it only senders' names and addresses, Gmail's category, and how many emails came and were opened, never what they say.
  • If Adam turns on read-aloud, answers are spoken with Microsoft Edge's built-in speech, which uses Microsoft's speech service. It's off unless he turns it on.
  • Nobody else. Google data isn't shared with anyone beyond the above.

Sign-in and removal

Jarvis's Google sign-in is stored on Adam's computer only, in a folder that's never copied online. It can be removed at any time from the Google Account's third-party connections page, or with Jarvis's own sign-out command, which asks Google to revoke it.

Google's Limited Use requirements

Jarvis's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Changes and contact

If how Jarvis handles data changes, this page changes first. Questions go to the support email shown on Jarvis's Google sign-in screen.
